{# The refraction filter, referenced from glass.css by url(#radar-refract). Why it is inline and not a separate .svg file: a filter referenced across documents is not honoured by any current browser, and one referenced from shadow DOM is not either. It has to be in this document. Why width/height are 0 and not display:none: a filter whose element is display:none is never resolved, so backdrop-filter: url() would silently do nothing. The filter is built as a real optical chain rather than a single blur: feTurbulence at a low baseFrequency + feGaussianBlur -> a smooth, non-repeating displacement field. A PNG would have to be downloaded and would visibly tile; this costs nothing and never repeats. feDisplacementMap -> bends the backdrop along that field. --glass-refract caps it at 8 because past about 12 the edge stops reading as glass and starts reading as a bug. three feColorMatrix + feDisplacementMap + feBlend -> splits the channels by a fraction of a pixel and recombines them, which is where the faint red/cyan fringe along a hard edge comes from. Every filter primitive reads the SourceGraphic, so nothing accumulates and the result is deterministic rather than depending on evaluation order. Safari and Firefox do not support backdrop-filter: url() at all. They fall back to the plain frosted material in glass.css, which is a complete design in its own right -- see the note at the top of that file. #} {# id is read by theme.js so "reduce motion" can call pauseAnimations() on this document. SMIL cannot be switched off from a CSS media query, and a user who asked for less motion should not get a permanently drifting refraction. #} {# Edge-lens refraction: two filters that push the backdrop into a different shape in the centre vs near the rim. The physical intuition is a pane of curved glass: the centre looks almost straight through, but near the edge the glass acts like a lens and bends what is behind it. That is what makes an iOS surface read as liquid: the bend is concentrated at the rim, the centre is calm, and the eye stops looking for a flat texture. How the edge-only modulation is built: T = a moving fractal-noise field, used as the raw displacement map (its R/G channels later drive feDisplacementMap). M = a soft edge-only mask: a rounded-rectangle STROKE rasterised by feImage, then blurred so the rim is a smooth ramp instead of a hard band. The stroke uses the same rx ratio as the glass surface so the mask lines up with the visible border. preserveAspectRatio is "none" on purpose: the mask has to cover every direction of the bounding box, and a slight stretch on long thin cards is far less noticeable than a missing rim. D = the displacement map actually used. D = T*M - 0.5*M + 0.5 is the closed form of "no shift in the centre, full field near the rim". Written as an feComposite arithmetic primitive so the four scalars k1..k4 encode the formula directly; no scripting, no second pass. Two strengths are exported: #radar-refract nav and panel -- strong rim, tighter transition. #radar-refract-soft card -- gentle rim, wider transition, so a row of cards does not feel like a row of fish-eyes. Both filters still carry the channel-split chromatic fringe and the saturation/brightness lift, because both effects are themselves strongest near the rim and quietly disappear in the centre, which is exactly how real glass behaves. Safari and Firefox do not support backdrop-filter: url() at all. They fall back to the plain frosted material in glass.css, which is a complete design in its own right -- see the note at the top of that file. #}
